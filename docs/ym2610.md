# YM2610 / YM2610B (OPNB) notes

The YM2610 is an OPN core with four FM channels, the AY SSG and two sample
engines. The YM2610B has six FM channels. Furnace calls the four-channel
part `YM2610` (system id `0xa5`, 14 channels) and the six-channel part
`YM2610B` (`0x9e`, 16 channels).

## VGM stream

| What | Where |
| --- | --- |
| Clock | header `0x4C`; bit 31 selects the B variant, bit 30 a second instance |
| Port 0 | command `0x58`, second instance `0xA8` |
| Port 1 | command `0x59`, second instance `0xA9` |
| ADPCM-A ROM | data block `0x82` |
| ADPCM-B ROM | data block `0x83` (the VGM spec calls it delta-T) |

Port 0 holds the SSG (`0x00-0x0F`), ADPCM-B (`0x10-0x1C`) and the first FM
channels. Port 1 holds ADPCM-A (`0x00-0x2D`) and the remaining FM channels.
`vgm.py` stores port 1 as `0x100 | register` and splits the SSG writes into
`ym2610_ssg`.

Furnace's VGM export writes the ADPCM-A bank as block `0x82` and the
ADPCM-B bank as block `0x83`. The source uses `0x82` for ADPCM-A and
`0x83` for ADPCM-B when that block is present. Extract B from `0x83` and
fall back to `0x82` only when the source has no `0x83`. Tools that read an
exported module must use `0x83` for ADPCM-B.

## Channel layout

```
4 FM    0-3     or 0-5 on the B variant
PSG     4-6     or 6-8
ADPCM-A 7-12    or 9-14
ADPCM-B 13      or 15
```

The four-channel FM register map is two channels per port:

| Channel | Port | Offset | `0x28` value |
| --- | --- | --- | --- |
| FM 1 | 0 | 1 | 1 |
| FM 2 | 0 | 2 | 2 |
| FM 3 | 1 | 1 | 5 |
| FM 4 | 1 | 2 | 6 |

The B variant is three channels per port: offsets `0,1,2` on each port and
`0x28` values `0,1,2` then `4,5,6`.

## FM pitch

Furnace runs the YM2610 platform in linear pitch mode with a `fmFreqBase`
of `9440540`. `ym2610_note_for` inverts that formula. The YM2612's base is
`9437184`, and the two must not share a constant. Carrier MULT relabelling
uses the same rule as the YM2612: the note follows the loudest carrier and
a constant pitch macro cancels the move.

## SSG

The SSG is the AY model with a divider of 32. The YM2203 uses 16. The
register effects are the ordinary `20xx`, `21xx`, `22xy`, `23xx` and `24xx`
forms on the three PSG channels.

## ADPCM-A

Six channels at a fixed rate of `clock / 432`. The register map:

| Register | Meaning |
| --- | --- |
| `0x100` | key-on/off strobe |
| `0x101` | master volume, `0x3F` loudest |
| `0x108-0x10D` | channel volume (5 bits) and pan (bits 7/6) |
| `0x110-0x115` | start address / 256 low |
| `0x118-0x11D` | start address / 256 high |
| `0x120-0x125` | end address / 256 low |
| `0x128-0x12D` | end address / 256 high |

`0x100` is a strobe. A write with bit 7 clear retriggers every selected
channel, including one that is already sounding. A write with bit 7 set
stops the selected channels. The driver often writes `0xC0` (volume zero)
before the key-on and the real channel level a few samples later. The
collector updates an active hit whose `0x108-0x10D` write lands within 64
samples of the gate. Later `0x101` and `0x108-0x10D` writes update the
pattern level and pan while the sample plays. A final pan-off becomes a
note-off at that row, since the pattern volume cannot mute the channel
completely. A gate whose pan is 0 and never returns is silent for its
whole life and is skipped.

A hit selects a 256-byte-aligned range in the `0x82` ROM. The writer keeps
one sample body per distinct `(start, end)` range and maps up to 120 of
them into one note-map instrument per group. The instrument type is
`DIV_INS_ADPCMA` (37), the sample depth is ADPCM-A (5). The pattern note
only selects a sample slot. The rate is fixed by the chip.

The writer gives every A gate an explicit note-off. An off that would
share its row with the next key-on is redundant because that key-on
retriggers the chip. A different sample whose trigger shares a row moves
to the next free row; a same-sample repeat within a row keeps the later
start. A gate still sounding at the loop wrap gets no off when its source
stop lies past the wrap: the player jumps before that stop, and the sample
must continue into the next iteration. The loop cut is the header's start
plus length. A gate past the wrap stays in the next iteration instead of
playing before the jump.

## ADPCM-B

One channel. The start/end registers are also address / 256. The playback
rate is

```
rate_hz = clock / 144 * delta_n / 65535
```

Furnace's default `oldCenterRate` is 8363 Hz while its linear-pitch C-4
reference is 8372 Hz. The stored sample center rate is multiplied by
`8363 / 8372`, and without that correction the exported rate register is about
0.1 percent high. The channel volume is 8-bit, and the pattern volume column
carries 0-255 here. The source commonly writes the pan two samples after
the start bit, and the collector folds a `0x11` write within 64 samples
into the hit. Later `0x11`, `0x19`/`0x1A` and `0x1B` writes while the
sample plays become row effects: delta-N turns into E5/E8/E9 pitch steps
and the level into volume-column cells. A sample that is retuned, faded or
panned mid-hit then keeps the source's motion. A final pan-off stops the
note. A write followed by a gate within 64 samples is the next hit's setup
and is ignored. Furnace loops the whole sample. The instrument type is
`DIV_INS_ADPCMB` (38), the sample depth is ADPCM-B (6). The payload comes
from the source's `0x83` ROM. The `0x82` block holds ADPCM-A; using it for B
silences the channel and makes the audit compare zero to zero.

The writer applies the same rule to B: a gate whose source stop is past the
wrap keeps sounding across the jump, and a gate before the wrap stays on the
last loop row. When the source does stop it before the wrap, the export
writes the `0x10` reset bit on that row.

## Verification

`diag.py` compares the FM f-num/block and the SSG tone period. ADPCM
channels have no pitch stream. A sample audit compares every source
ADPCM-A/B gate with the Furnace export by channel and time, then checks
the extracted payload, range length, volume, pan and, for ADPCM-B, the
rate register. Gates that are panned off for their whole life are silent
and left out. The B side reads the source `0x83` bank and the export's
`0x83` bank. An extraction from the wrong bank cannot pass. The YM2610
tests also assert the note-off cells in the module and the exported stop
registers (`0x10` bit 0 for B, `0x100` bit 7 for A).

Three reference packs, converted with `--no-normalize`:

| Pack | Tracks | Pitch-divergent tracks | ADPCM-A matched | ADPCM-B matched |
| --- | --- | --- | --- | --- |
| Night Striker | 21 | 1 (815 ms, 10 Brain Worker) | 5,929 / 5,929 | 2,662 / 2,662 |
| Viewpoint | 14 | 0 | 5,892 / 5,892 | 595 / 595 |
| Metal Slug 3 | 29 | 1 (88 ms, 05 Marine Diver) | 47,856 / 47,856 | 4,570 / 4,570 |

Every audible ADPCM gate is matched in all three packs, and every match
reproduces its payload, range length, volume, pan and rate exactly. The
Metal Slug 3 A total leaves out 295 gates that are panned off for their
whole life. A few export gates have no source partner (3, 7 and 2 in the
packs, plus 1 B gate each); they are same-row retriggers the source
already folds into the previous hit.

The two pitch residuals are row-boundary artifacts, not wrong notes. Brain
Worker's source glides by about eight f-num units per frame and the export
steps at row boundaries. Marine Diver keys channel 2 before writing its
f-num. The source holds a stale pitch for about 95 ms at the start while
the export ramps to the real pitch.

## Known limits

- Channel-2 special and CSM modes are converted with the base layout. The
  four FM3 operator columns and the CSM timer channel are not emitted.
- The ADPCM-B payload is copied from the source's `0x83` block without
  transcoding. The VGM block is named delta-T, but the source's `0x11`
  bit 1 selects 4-bit ADPCM. A source that sets 8-bit delta-T mode would
  need a transcode.
- A source without a `0x82` block still gets the system, but its ADPCM-A
  channels have no samples; ADPCM-B can still use `0x83`.
